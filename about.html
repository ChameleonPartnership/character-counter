Who maintains it

Character Counter Pro is maintained by Chameleon Partnership. The site exists because character counting is a small task that sits inside serious work: campaign approvals, product launches, app store submissions, metadata reviews, support copy and forms used by customers.

The tool is intentionally direct. Paste text, review the numbers, revise the draft and copy the statistics if a ticket or checklist needs them. There is no account, no project dashboard and no server-side text analysis.

What makes it different

The counter runs in the browser. Text typed or pasted into the main tool is processed locally for characters, words, lines, sentences, paragraphs, reading time and platform-limit progress. Character Counter Pro does not need to receive the text to calculate those numbers.

That matters for professional copy teams. Drafts often include unreleased offers, product names, internal positioning, customer examples or support scenarios. A privacy-first counter lets teams check length without sending that draft to another service.

The same principle shapes the content around the tool. The cheat sheet gives working limits for common platforms, and the blog explains how to use those limits in real editing decisions. The site is not trying to become a publishing suite. It is a focused reference and counter for the moment when a draft has to fit.

Who it serves

The site is built for copywriters, editors, marketers, SEO leads, product managers, UX writers, developers, students and support teams. The shared need is simple: write within a limit while keeping the message clear.

Character Counter Pro is also meant to be useful before a draft is ready for public systems. A campaign subject line, app store subtitle, support reply or product error message may contain details that are not yet published. A local browser workflow gives teams a quick check without adding another account or upload step.

The blog and cheat sheet add context around the tool. They explain the limits behind common channels and show how to shorten copy without removing the details that make it useful. For questions, email paulodonnell@chameleonpartnership.com.
