What it does and how to use it

The lorem ipsum generator creates placeholder words, sentences or paragraphs for layouts, prototypes, CMS models and design QA. Choose an output type, set the count and copy the result. The generated text is intentionally neutral so it does not look like final copy.

Use placeholder text when the layout needs volume but the approved words are not ready. Replace it before publishing. Lorem ipsum is useful in drafts precisely because it is not meaningful content.

Reference notes for placeholder text

Placeholder text helps teams test shape. A card with a two-word heading behaves differently from a card with a 58-character heading. A layout with three short paragraphs may look calm, while the real CMS entry has one long paragraph and a list. Generating measured filler lets designers and developers test spacing, wrapping and overflow before final copy arrives.

There is a risk, though. Lorem ipsum can hide content problems because nobody reads it. For early layout checks, it is fine. For final QA, switch to realistic draft copy with real numbers, product names and actions. A pricing page should be tested with prices. A help article should be tested with steps. A form should be tested with the longest labels likely to appear in production.

Use paragraph counts for article templates, sentence counts for cards and modules, and word counts for tiny fields. If a component has a strict character limit, generate a close approximation and then use the character counter to test exact strings. Placeholder text should help reveal constraints, not become a substitute for writing.

Worked placeholder example

If a designer needs to test a three-card feature row, generating three one-sentence blocks is better than pasting the same sentence into every card. Repeated filler can hide spacing problems because every card wraps in the same way. Varied placeholder sentences reveal whether one card becomes taller and pushes the layout out of alignment.

For CMS modelling, generate both short and long samples. A teaser field might look good with 12 words and fail with 35. A two-paragraph article intro might fit on desktop and feel too heavy on mobile. Placeholder text is most useful when it tests the edges of a component, not only the perfect middle case.

Quality checks before publishing

After using the tool, do one final human check in the destination context. Text that is technically valid can still be awkward when it appears inside a button, search result, app store field, spreadsheet, social preview or code review. Check the longest realistic version, including names, dates, tracking codes, translated words and any suffixes added by the publishing system.

Keep a short note of the final count when the work needs approval. A note such as "title, 57 characters" or "script, 690 spoken words" gives reviewers a concrete constraint and reduces subjective back-and-forth. The tool gives the measurement, but the publishing context decides whether the result is actually good.

Frequently asked questions

Does this tool upload my text?

No. The tool runs in your browser with vanilla JavaScript. Your pasted text is used on the page for the current task and is not submitted to a Character Counter Pro server.

Can I use it on a phone?

Yes. The controls are designed to stack on small screens, labels remain visible, and results update with keyboard, touch and paste input.

Should I still check the final platform?

Yes. These tools are editing aids. Always test final copy in the CMS, social composer, app store, email platform or codebase before publishing.
